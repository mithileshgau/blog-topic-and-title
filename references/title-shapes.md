# Title shapes

## Check this yourself rather than trusting the table

The shape that wins is a property of the query, not of your taste or your
house style. To find it for any keyword:

1. Search the keyword
2. Write down the top ten organic titles
3. Classify each: question, listicle, how-to, statement, announcement
4. Write in the shape that dominates

Ten minutes, and it settles arguments that otherwise run for weeks.

## What this usually shows

- **Definitional queries** — roughly half the ranking titles are
  question-shaped. "What Is X?" is correct here, and this is the one case
  where a question title is the strongest choice
- **Tool-selection queries** — lists dominate and questions are often absent
  entirely. A question title will not rank
- **Build queries** — statements with a colon and an enumerated promise
- **Troubleshooting queries** — "Why X happens" or "Fixing X"

## The colon-enumeration subtitle

Common among pages that rank. It front-loads the term, then promises coverage:

- `X: Definition, Components and Limits`
- `X: Patterns, Costs and When Not To`
- `X on Y: Architecture and a Working Example`

## Rewrites

| Weak | Stronger | Why |
|---|---|---|
| How Do I Build a Multi-Agent System? | What Is a Multi-Agent System? Patterns, Costs and When Not To | Prompt is definitional; term moves to the front |
| Which Monitoring Tools Should I Use? | 12 Monitoring Tools for X in 2026 | Selection prompts want a list |
| How Do I Sandbox Untrusted Code? | Sandboxing Untrusted Code: What Runs Where | Term front-loaded, "How Do I" removed |
| The Model Is Not the Product | What Actually Breaks When You Ship | A thesis is unfindable; the question is searchable |

## Checks

- Under 60 characters
- Contains the searched phrase word for word
- Does not start with "How Do I"
- Year only on selection posts
- Different in shape from the last three posts published
